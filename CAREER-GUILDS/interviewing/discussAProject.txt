Interviewing: Discussing a Project
1. The Value of Discussing Past Projects
In technical interviews, discussing past projects is the most effective way to demonstrate that you have the skills and knowledge to handle complex work. A strong project discussion proves:

You understand project structure and how components fit together.

You possess a solid grasp of varied technical skills and concepts.

You communicate complex technical information effectively.

2. How to Introduce and Frame the Project
Because interview time is limited, you must structure your discussion efficiently.

Lead with the Challenge: Begin by highlighting the biggest technical challenge you encountered. This immediately engages the interviewer and sets the context for your story.

Establish Context: Describe the application domain, the basic need the project fulfilled, and why you chose to discuss this specific project.

Outline the Structure: Mention the specific modules and describe the overall flow or process of the project.

Define Success: Explain the main functionality, the advantages of your solution, and whether the project was ultimately successful or not (and why). Note: Use graphs or flowcharts if they help clarify the structure.

3. Detailing Tools and Technologies
Explaining your tech stack allows the interviewer to gauge your professional skills and the working process of the project.

Categorize Your Tech: Organize your information into functional categories to make it digestible. Examples include: User Interface (UI), Application Logic/Business Services, External Services, Database, and DevOps processes.

Prepare for Deep Dives: Interviewers will likely ask deeper questions about specific tools. Review your tools, technologies, and third-party documentation ahead of time so you can confidently answer technical questions regarding the project's structure.

4. Highlighting Your Specific Accomplishments
Ensure the interviewer understands exactly what you contributed, not just what the team achieved.

Your Code and Tasks: Detail your specific tasks, the exact code you wrote, its function, and the obstacles you overcame.

Problem-Solving Focus: Emphasize your ability to tackle problems independently, develop creative solutions, and utilize resources for help when needed.

Professional Growth: Detail how the experience helped you grow as a developer, particularly your ability to handle negative feedback and confront unexpected challenges.

Target Alignment: Whenever possible, explicitly align the skills you used in this project with the specific skills required by the role you are interviewing for.

5. Proving Reliability: Testing and Follow-Up
Demonstrate your commitment to keeping software reliable by explaining how you verified your work.

Testing Strategies: Describe how you approached testing feature specifications to ensure needs were met. Detail your use of both automated and manual tests.

Monitoring and Validation: Discuss the specific procedures or tools you used to monitor code, log failures, or analyze the codebase for common mistakes in production.

6. Summarizing Personal Learning
Conclude your project discussion by succinctly but thoroughly summarizing your takeaways.

Hard Skills: Mention the new technologies and technical skills you learned.

Soft Skills: Discuss improvements in problem-solving, solution design, and teamwork.

Embrace Mistakes: Analyze what you learned from mistakes or unsuccessful work just as much as you discuss your successes.
