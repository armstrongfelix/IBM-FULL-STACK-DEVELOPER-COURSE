Expert Viewpoints: Negotiating an Offer
1. The Right Mindset for Negotiation
Overcome the Fear: Many candidates fear losing a job offer if they try to negotiate. Remember that negotiation is simply two parties trying to reach a mutual agreement. If a company revokes an offer merely because you attempted to negotiate, it is likely not a place you want to work.

Leverage Mutual Interest: The company has already decided they want you on their team. Use this fact to build your confidence.

The Approach: Always start with gratitude and excitement (e.g., "Thank you so much for the offer. I'm so excited to work for the team."). Then, respectfully state what you would like to negotiate, such as base salary or restricted stock units.

Finding the Middle Ground: The company may accommodate your request fully, or they may offer a compromise (e.g., "I can't do X, but I can do Y"). View it as a collaborative effort to reach a consensus.

2. Preparation and Non-Negotiables
Start Early: Preparation for negotiation should begin before you even apply for the job.

Identify Non-Negotiables: Write down what is absolutely critical to you. This could include salary, work location, working hours, flexibility, or career progression.

Gather Information: Discuss these priorities with your recruiter and interviewers during the interview process so you understand what the company can provide before an offer is even made.

Determine Trade-offs: By the time you receive an offer, decide what you are willing to compromise on. For example, if the company cannot offer the exact flexibility you want, determine what financial figure would make up for that loss.

Build Your Case: Go into the negotiation armed with specific reasons why you are worth the counteroffer. Present a compelling case based on the specific skills and value you bring to the team.

3. Salary Negotiation Tactics
Let Them Go First: Always let the company make the initial offer. It is generally best not to be the first one to state a number.

Do Your Research: Investigate the standard salary range for the specific position based on the required technical skills and your current level of experience.

Set a Minimum: Determine the absolute bottom number you are willing to accept before walking away.

Aim Higher: Make your counteroffer slightly higher than your minimum acceptable number. Companies will often meet you in the middle, landing you safely at or above your baseline.

Advocate for Yourself: Do not undervalue yourself simply because you are inexperienced or new to the field. Self-confidence is crucial, and you should not accept an offer that is offensively low for your skill set.
