Expert Viewpoints: Job Interview Prep
1. General Preparation and Research
Preparation is key, and interviewers can easily tell when a candidate has not prepared. While you do not need to research absolutely everything about the company, you should take the following steps:

Review the LinkedIn profile of the person who will be interviewing you.

Look at the company's latest press releases and company reports.

Come prepared with relevant questions and criteria to ask the business.

2. The Necessity of Studying
Because the interview process is lengthy and rigorous, the most important advice for any candidate is to study intentionally.

A Universal Requirement: This applies to both new engineers and experienced developers.

Interview vs. Daily Work: Technical interviews often test concepts (like specific algorithms) that developers do not use every single day in their actual jobs, making intentional studying absolutely necessary.

3. Creating a Technical Study Plan
Tailor Your Prep: Once you know the kind of role you are going for, create a specific study plan.

Platform Practice: Use platforms like HackerRank or LeetCode to practice problem-solving questions.

Core Concepts: Ensure you are completely up-to-date on your data structures and algorithms.

Consistency is Key: Working consistently (e.g., a little every day) is much better than cramming (e.g., doing five problems one day and only one the next week). Constant exposure reduces interview anxiety and gets you used to the style of questions.

4. Communication During Technical Interviews
Being able to solve a problem is important, but communicating your process is an equally vital skill.

Talk Out Loud: Practice talking through problems out loud at home. During the actual interview, avoid being silent while you work. Walk the interviewer through your process from point A to point B.

Getting Unstuck: If you get stuck, narrating your thoughts lets the interviewer know where your mind is. Because interviewers are often helpful, hearing your thought process allows them to offer hints.

Showing Your Logic: Even if you do not answer the question 100% correctly, talking out loud proves how you process information and take steps to move forward.

5. Practicing Verbal Delivery
Beyond technical coding questions, you must practice how you convey your overall knowledge.

Mock Questions: Have someone ask you questions out loud (even non-technical ones) to practice your delivery.

Key Topics to Rehearse: Practice exactly how you are going to talk about the projects you have created, the impact you had on previous teams, and the technologies you know.

Mindset: Remember that practice makes progression.
