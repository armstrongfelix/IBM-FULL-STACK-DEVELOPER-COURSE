Lecture Notes: Company and Industry Research
1. Researching via the Company Website
The best way to prepare for a job interview is to learn as much as possible about the organization and the industry. A company’s website is a rich primary source of information. When reviewing it, pay special attention to:

History: Look for the company’s origins, mission, and vision statements to understand its goals and culture.

Keywords: Notice repeating words used to describe what the company does or represents. Use this specific language during your interview to show you align with their desired image.

Careers: Reviewing other job listings reveals where the company plans to expand and highlights its current priorities.

Data: Pay close attention to quantitative information, which can provide clues about the specific tools and technologies the company uses.

2. Additional Sources for Company Research
To gain a complete picture, expand your research beyond the official website using these sources:

Local Organizations: Check the local Chamber of Commerce or the Better Business Bureau website.

Social Media & Web Searches: Look for current news, featured articles, and cultural insights. You may discover information that confirms your interest or makes you reconsider the company.

LinkedIn: Gather broader company information and look up your specific interviewer to understand their professional background before you meet.

Review Sites (e.g., Glassdoor): Read recent posts from employees and customers to learn about their firsthand experiences. You can often find interview reports to help you anticipate what to expect.

3. The Informational Interview
An informational interview is an informal meeting with a professional currently working in the field you want to pursue. It is strictly for researching careers, gathering tips, and getting expert advice—it is not a job interview.

Finding and Scheduling an Interview:

Make a list of 3 to 5 companies you want to learn about, ideally in your area.

Use LinkedIn, professional networks, social media, teachers, or friends to identify professionals in roles like Software Engineer.

Reach out via email, phone, or LinkedIn with a brief introduction and a clear statement of your purpose for meeting.

Conducting the Interview:

Allow the conversation to flow naturally and be open to unexpected information.

Take notes, specifically capturing expert advice and actionable tips.

Respect the professional's time: check your progress halfway through your questions to ensure you stay within the agreed-upon time frame. Keep the meeting to 30 minutes or less.

Post-Interview Actions:

Review your notes and highlight any points that require further research.

Send a thank-you note to the person you interviewed.

Connect on LinkedIn or other platforms to maintain the relationship and expand your network.

4. Suggested Questions for an Informational Interview
Prepare questions that fit within a 30-minute window. Good questions typically fall into three categories:

History and Background:

How did you become interested in your role?

How did you prepare to enter the field?

How did you begin your career?

Current Role:

What is a typical workday like for you?

What do you like most about your work?

What is one of your favorite projects that you have worked on?

What is the most difficult challenge you have dealt with?

What would you like to accomplish next in your career?

Actionable Career Advice:

What related fields or career paths would you recommend I also look into?

How do you stay current and learn new technology?

What current issues and trends in the field should I be aware of?

What are the most effective strategies for seeking a position in this field?
