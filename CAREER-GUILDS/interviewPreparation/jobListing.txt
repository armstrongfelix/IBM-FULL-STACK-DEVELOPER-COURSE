Lecture Notes: Assessing Job Listings
1. Anatomy of a Job Listing
Most job listings are divided into several key sections that provide crucial information for applicants:

Overview of the Company and Role: A broad description of the hiring company and the specific job position.

Responsibilities and Tasks: Outlines what the employee is expected to do, what they will be in charge of, how they will function within the company, and who they will work with.

Requirements and Qualifications: Details what the company is looking for, including education, certifications, experience, and specific hard and soft skills.

Required vs. Preferred: Ideally, you should meet all "required" qualifications and at least 50% of the "preferred" ones.

Strategy: Identify missing requirements and determine if you have other skills or experiences that compensate for them.

2. Full-Time (FTE) vs. Contract Positions
Whether a job is listed as full-time (FTE) or contract (contingent/temporary) refers to the worker's tax status, not their working hours (contractors often work full-time hours).

Full-Time Employees (FTE):

Expect to work indefinitely.

Paid regularly via salary or hourly wage.

Must comply with internal policies, trainings, and work procedures.

Entitled to receive company benefits and incentives.

Usually possess different legal rights regarding labor practices and termination.

Contract or Contingent Workers:

Hired for a specific, predetermined length of time.

Paid hourly or by specific deliverables.

Receive minimal to no training; they are expected to start with the necessary skills.

Generally do not receive company benefits or incentives.

Can often be fired without cause or notice and lack certain workplace protections.

3. Navigating Application Policies
Companies have highly specific application processes. Failing to follow their exact instructions can immediately disqualify your application.

Submission Methods: Many companies post on external job boards but require you to apply directly through their own website. Others may require you to email a specific person or team.

Additional Materials: Beyond a resume, pay close attention to whether the employer requires a cover letter, work samples, a portfolio link, screening questions, or skill assessments. Make sure you understand exactly how and what to submit before applying.

4. Recognizing Problematic Job Listings (Red Flags)
A job listing is a company's first impression and an indicator of its culture and competence. Watch out for these warning flags before applying:

Lack of Clarity: Vague job titles or descriptions suggest unclear role boundaries. Overuse of jargon and acronyms often points to poor internal communication.

Unrealistic Expectations: Listings requiring several years of experience for an "entry-level" job, or requiring high-level expertise in too many distinct areas. This indicates the company either does not understand the role or plans to exploit new hires and set them up for failure.

Evident Biases or Prejudice: Any language that explicitly excludes or states a preference for candidates based on irrelevant personal traits (e.g., race, religion, gender, age, orientation, nationality, marital/parental status).
