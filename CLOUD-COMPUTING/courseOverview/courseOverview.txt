Course Overview: Introduction to Cloud Computing
Core Objective
This course is designed to build a strong foundational understanding of cloud computing. It bridges the gap between high-level business strategy and technical execution, preparing learners to either make informed business decisions or step into a technical role as a Cloud Practitioner.

Target Audience & Prerequisites
Who it is for: The curriculum accommodates everyone from non-technical business leaders (C-level executives, managers, sellers, students) to aspiring technical professionals (developers, systems/cloud architects, technical leads, data/security engineers, consultants).

Prerequisites: Basic computer literacy is the only requirement. Absolutely no prior knowledge of computer programming or cloud computing is necessary.

Curriculum Breakdown
The course material is divided into four main learning pillars:

1. Cloud Fundamentals & Business Value

Definition & Characteristics: The core definition of what cloud computing is and the essential traits that define a cloud environment.

Context & Future: The historical evolution of cloud technology and current emerging trends.

The Business Case: Understanding the financial, operational, and strategic reasons why organizations adopt cloud computing.

2. Architecture & Delivery Models

Service Models: How cloud services are delivered (typically covering Infrastructure as a Service, Platform as a Service, and Software as a Service).

Deployment Models: Where and how cloud environments are hosted (such as Public, Private, or Hybrid clouds).

Key Components: The underlying architectural building blocks that make cloud environments work.

3. The Cloud Industry Landscape

Major Providers: An introduction to the prominent cloud service providers dominating the current market and the specific services they offer.

Real-World Application: Case studies exploring how different industry verticals successfully utilize cloud computing.

Career Pathways: An overview of the job market and specific career opportunities available within the cloud computing domain.

4. Hands-On Practical Experience

Platform: IBM Cloud.

Activity: Learners will move beyond theory by creating an active account and provisioning a real object storage instance to upload and store files on the cloud.
