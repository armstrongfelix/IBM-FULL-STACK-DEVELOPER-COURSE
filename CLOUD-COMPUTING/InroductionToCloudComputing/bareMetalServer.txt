Bare Metal Servers: A Comprehensive Overview

A bare metal server is a dedicated, single-tenant physical server.

Unlike a virtual server that shares hardware with other users, a bare metal server is reserved exclusively for one customer.

The cloud provider physically installs the server into a data center rack and hands over access to the user.

Shared Responsibilities

The cloud provider manages the server's physical components up to the Operating System (OS).

This means they are responsible for fixing or replacing hardware, maintaining the network connection to the rack, and performing hard reboots if the physical machine fails.

The customer takes on the responsibility for administering and managing everything else on the server, including the OS, applications, and software configurations.

Configuration and Flexibility

Bare metal servers offer deep customization.

They can be pre-configured by the cloud provider to meet specific workload packages or custom-configured according to the customer's exact specifications for processors, RAM, hard drives, and specialized components.

Customers have the freedom to install their own operating systems and can even install custom hypervisors to create their own virtual machines and VM farms, bypassing the provider's standard virtualization offerings.

Users can also integrate Graphics Processing Units (GPUs).

This is highly beneficial for accelerating scientific computations, rendering professional-grade graphics, and powering intensive data analytics.

Provisioning Time and Cost

Because bare metal servers are actual physical machines, they take significantly longer to provision than virtual servers.

Pre-configured builds generally take 20 to 40 minutes, while highly customized builds can take three to four hours depending on the cloud provider.

Since these resources are dedicated exclusively to a single client at all times, bare metal servers tend to be more expensive than similarly sized virtual machines.

It is also important to note that not all major cloud providers offer bare metal options.

Performance and Ideal Use Cases

Because there is no underlying hypervisor layer and no sharing of hardware with other tenants, clients have full access and control over the machine.

This makes bare metal servers ideal for long-term, high-performance use in highly secure and isolated environments.

These servers excel at fulfilling the demanding needs of High-Performance Computing (HPC) and data-intensive applications that require minimal latency.

Common workload examples perfectly suited for bare metal include Big Data analytics, AI and deep learning, Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP), Customer Relationship Management (CRM), and serving as the foundational hardware for custom virtualization.

Bare Metal vs. Virtual Servers

When deciding between a bare metal server and a virtual server, the most important considerations revolve around the specific needs of the customer.

Bare metal servers are the best choice for CPU and I/O intensive workloads.

They offer the absolute highest levels of consistent performance and security, satisfy strict compliance requirements, and provide complete hardware flexibility and transparency.

However, this comes with added management and operational overhead for the user.

Virtual servers, on the other hand, are rapidly provisioned in minutes, provide a highly elastic and scalable environment, and are generally lower in cost.

However, because they share underlying physical hardware with other tenants, they can sometimes face limitations in maximum throughput and consistent performance.
