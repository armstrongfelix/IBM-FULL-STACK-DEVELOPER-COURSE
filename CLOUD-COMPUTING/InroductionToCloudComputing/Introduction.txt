Why Choose Cloud Computing Over Traditional Hosting?
Cloud computing provides significant advantages over traditional, local server hosting by combining technologies like virtualization with modern delivery models. The primary benefits include:

Agility and Promptness: When a business requires more bandwidth or storage, cloud services can address the need almost immediately. This eliminates the need to wait for complex and costly hardware updates to traditional IT infrastructure.

Ultimate Flexibility: Users can customize and access applications and services from anywhere, provided they have an internet connection.

Cost and Maintenance Efficiency: Cloud computing eliminates much of the overhead associated with maintaining physical hardware, enabling the efficient deployment and marketing of applications regardless of maintenance costs.

The Official NIST Definition
To establish a common understanding, the industry relies on the definition provided by the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST):

"Cloud computing is a model for enabling convenient, on-demand network access to a shared pool of configurable computing resources (e.g., networks, servers, storage, applications, and services) that can be rapidly provisioned and released with minimal management effort or service provider interaction."

According to NIST, a true cloud computing model is composed of five essential characteristics, four deployment models, and three service models.

The 5 Essential Characteristics of Cloud Computing
For a service to be considered "cloud computing," it must exhibit the following five traits:

1. On-Demand Self-Service
Users can access and provision cloud resources whenever required without needing human intervention from the provider. Services are always available 365 days a year—much like a 24-hour ATM or vending machine—with no exceptions other than unforeseen outages or security breaches.

2. Broad Network Access
Cloud resources are accessible over a network using a wide variety of client platforms. Public cloud services can generally be accessed from anywhere using devices like desktops, laptops, tablets, smartphones, and wearables, provided there is internet connectivity. (In an on-premises private cloud, an internal Intranet is often sufficient).

3. Resource Pooling
The provider pools computing resources to serve multiple consumers simultaneously using a multi-tenant model. Resources are dynamically assigned and reassigned according to demand without the customer needing to worry about the physical location of those resources. This shared model gives providers economies of scale, making the cloud highly cost-efficient for consumers.

4. Rapid Elasticity
Capabilities can be scaled up (vertical scaling) or scaled out (horizontal scaling) rapidly based on demand. A classic example is an e-commerce site that automatically adds more server resources during a massive holiday traffic spike, and then dynamically decreases those resources once the sales period is over.

5. Measured Service
Cloud systems automatically control, optimize, and report resource usage. You only pay for what you use, which is known as a utility model of billing (similar to a monthly electric bill). It is important to note that this measured billing does not apply to free consumer services like Gmail and social media, or free-tier trial evaluations provided by platforms like AWS, Azure, or GCP.
