Choosing the Right Cloud Model: Expert Viewpoints

When an organization is deciding on the right cloud model, the decision largely depends on where the organization currently stands in its cloud journey, its specific business needs, the desired level of control, and budget considerations.

Infrastructure-as-a-Service (IaaS)

IaaS is often the right choice when an organization is facing strict time constraints. For example, if a data center lease is about to expire and there is no time to rewrite applications to be cloud-native, a business can simply take the application as it is and move it onto virtual machines in the cloud.

If your business simply needs a virtual machine—such as for hosting a website, creating virtual data centers, or performing data analysis—IaaS is the appropriate model.

While IaaS offers the highest level of control over how resources are configured, the tradeoff is that it requires the organization to take on the most management responsibilities. The business is responsible for managing the operating system, the application, and the overall virtual machine environment.

Furthermore, IaaS is generally more expensive and resource-intensive than other models, though it provides reliable and secure data storage at designated data centers.

Platform-as-a-Service (PaaS)

If an organization has more time to transition, it should consider re-platforming using PaaS. This involves moving applications to platforms like containers or services where the cloud provider operates and patches the underlying operating system.

PaaS is a perfect choice for software development companies. It provides a platform specifically designed for building products, supporting different programming languages, and facilitating easy team collaboration.

By abstracting away the details of the cloud infrastructure, developers can focus entirely on building and deploying new applications. All the user has to do is install extra dependencies.

In many cases, choosing PaaS saves money and investment while significantly accelerating product development and time-to-market.

Software-as-a-Service (SaaS)

Experts generally recommend staying as high on the cloud stack as possible to meet business requirements with the least amount of friction.

If a business needs out-of-the-box functionality—such as email, Customer Relationship Management (CRM) tools, or event management software—SaaS is the best option.

SaaS provides immediate business value with little to no technology resource investment beyond the cost of the subscription.

The vendor manages all software aspects, including automatic updates and security. It offers low-cost, easy adoption, and the ability to access the software from anywhere on any device.

Functions-as-a-Service (FaaS) / Serverless

If an organization is moving incredibly fast and writing a brand-new product from scratch, experts suggest starting with the latest approach: Functions-as-a-Service.

FaaS utilizes serverless functions, allowing teams to move with maximum speed because there is absolutely no underlying infrastructure to manage.

The cloud provider handles everything, including the security of the operating system, allowing developers to focus solely on writing the specific code functions needed for the application.

The Trade-off Principle

The core principle of choosing a model involves balancing control against responsibility.

As you move down the layers of abstraction—from SaaS, down to PaaS, and finally to IaaS—you gain significantly more control over how your resources and environment are configured.

However, you simultaneously take on a much heavier burden of management responsibilities and infrastructure maintenance.

For example, a developer might default to a SaaS option like WordPress for a simple website. If the website requires custom React app development, they might move down to PaaS. Only if the app requires a highly specific operating system not supported by the PaaS provider would they move further down to IaaS, taking that trade-off only when strictly necessary.
