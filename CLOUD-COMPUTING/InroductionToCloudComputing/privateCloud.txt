The Private Cloud Deployment Model: A Comprehensive Overview

The National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) defines a private cloud as a cloud infrastructure provisioned for the exclusive use of a single organization comprising multiple consumers (such as different internal business units).

A private cloud can be owned, managed, and operated by the organization itself, a third party, or a combination of both. Furthermore, it can exist either on-premises or off-premises.

Internal vs. External Private Clouds and VPCs

When a private cloud platform is provisioned over an organization's internal infrastructure, it runs on-premises and is owned, managed, and operated entirely by the organization.

When a private cloud is provisioned over a third-party cloud provider's infrastructure, it is known as an external private cloud. This specific offering is commonly referred to as a Virtual Private Cloud (VPC).

A VPC is a public cloud offering that allows an organization to establish its own secure, logically isolated, cloud-like computing environment within a shared public cloud. By using a VPC, organizations can leverage the dynamic scalability, high availability, and lower ownership costs of a public cloud, while maintaining infrastructure and security tailored to their unique needs. Major public cloud providers, such as Amazon Web Services and IBM, offer VPC solutions.

Core Philosophy and Benefits

Fundamentally, a private cloud is a virtualized environment designed to bring the benefits of a public cloud platform without the perceived disadvantages of an open, shared architecture.

Users of a private cloud, such as internal developers and business units, still get to leverage operational efficiencies, self-service provisioning, and economies of scale. However, the organization maintains full, granular control over access, security, and specific compliance requirements.

Key benefits of adopting a private cloud include:

Direct IT Control: The ability to leverage the agility of cloud computing using systems that remain under the direct management and perceived control of the organization's internal IT department.

Resource Optimization: The ability to better utilize existing internal computing resources and maximize past investments in hardware and software, thereby reducing costs.

Enhanced Scalability and Cloud Bursting: Achieving better scalability through virtualization, including the ability to utilize "cloud bursting"—temporarily leveraging public cloud instances to handle surges in traffic, and returning solely to the private cloud when the surge subsides.

Customized Security: Controlled access and greater security measures that can be strictly customized to meet specific organizational and regulatory needs.

Agility and Speed: The ability to expand and provision computing resources in a relatively short amount of time, providing much greater business agility.

Primary Drivers and Use Cases

Organizations typically opt for a private cloud when their applications provide a unique competitive advantage, when they face strict regulatory concerns, or when their data is highly sensitive and subject to governmental industry regulations.

Common use cases for a private cloud include:

Modernizing Legacy Systems: A private cloud provides an opportunity for organizations to modernize and unify their in-house and legacy applications. Moving these applications off dedicated hardware allows them to leverage modern compute resources and diverse cloud services.

Strategic Integration: Organizations use the private cloud to integrate their existing data and application services with public cloud services. This allows them to run heavy compute jobs securely on the private cloud while selectively exposing or pulling data to leverage specific public cloud tools, effectively opening their data centers to broader cloud ecosystems.

Application Portability: Using a private cloud gives organizations the flexibility to build applications anywhere and move them across environments without compromising their baseline security and compliance standards.

Overcoming Regulatory Blockers: For organizations restricted from moving to a public cloud due to data sensitivity or regulatory compliance, a private cloud offers the operational benefits of on-demand enterprise resources while keeping critical data entirely within a dedicated, heavily controlled environment.
